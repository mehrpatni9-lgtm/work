System architecture

My chief of staff. What it actually does.

Not a chatbot I ask things. A working system with its own memory, its own research, and a rule that it checks before it ships.

01

I ask for something

A job to apply for, a company to understand, a deck to build, an email to write.

↓
02

It reads its own rules first

Everything I have ever corrected it on. How I write, what I will not claim, which numbers are confirmed and which are not. It starts from what it already knows about me.

↓
03

It researches

Crawls the site, pulls the public sitemap, checks every claim against the live page, and writes the findings to a file with a source on each line.

↓
04

It builds

The CV, the tailored version of it, the deck, the tracker, the site. All rendered from one profile file, so a number changed once is changed everywhere.

↓
05

It checks itself, and refuses

Twenty-eight labelled cases with deliberate traps. Below ninety per cent accuracy the build stops. It also tells me which claims it could not verify, instead of quietly filling the gap.

↓
06

I approve, then it ships

Nothing is sent, published or posted without me. Drafts only, every time.

Loop back to 01

It asks before it guesses

When two readings of a request would produce different work, it stops and asks rather than picking one and hoping.

Loop back to 02

Every correction becomes a rule

Tell it once that a phrase is not how I talk, and it is written into memory. It does not need telling twice.

Built in Claude Code. The research, the build pipeline and the eval suite are separate pieces that hand off to each other, which is why any one of them can be replaced without breaking the rest.
