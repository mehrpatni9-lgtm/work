Case study · Ivory

Building the room before the product

We were about to launch a preventative health product into a market that did not know it had a problem. So we did not launch it. We built the audience first.

The problem

Preventative products have no urgency. Nobody searches for a brain assessment. If we shipped first and marketed second, we would be selling to an empty room.

What I did

  • Started with caregivers, not patients. People looking after someone with Alzheimer's or dementia, plus seniors who wanted to avoid the same path.
  • Made the ask mutual. Join to help each other, and bring someone you know who is also a caregiver. The invitation was the growth loop.
  • Gave first, for months. Interactive sessions, webinars, habit building, sleep and routine. A wellness community, not a waiting list.
  • Introduced the assessment last. Only once the community had real value without it.

Where the leads came from

Top
Caregivers bringing caregivers. Member-led acquisition, so cost per member fell as the community grew.
Middle
Webinars and habit-building sessions, which turned members into people who understood why screening matters.
Middle
The community itself as an owned list, not a rented audience on a platform.
Bottom
The cognitive assessment, offered to a room that already trusted us.
3,000 members before the product shipped.

Launched into a room, not a void. And the community kept generating assessment leads long after launch week.

What to take from it

A community is a lead engine you own outright. The discipline is resisting the urge to sell into it early, because the moment it feels like a funnel, it stops being one.
