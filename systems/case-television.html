Selling a test nobody wanted to take

Brain health is stigmatised. In India, an estimated 91% of cases go undiagnosed. We had an FDA-registered cognitive assessment and people still would not take it.

The problem

A clinical assessment is not a product people want. It is a thing people avoid, and the category had no language that was not frightening.

What I did

  • Reframed the test as a habit. Built with neuroscientists, gamified across attention, reasoning and memory.
  • Capped usage at ten minutes a day. It cost engagement and bought the one thing a health brand cannot buy back.
  • Refused fear-based selling. Preventative, educational, habit first. No scare copy, ever.
  • Moved screening into the routine checkup. Something you do alongside blood work, not after you notice you are forgetting.

Where the leads came from

Top
Performance ads and long-form content on what mild cognitive impairment is.
Middle
Webinars. The best converting asset we had.
Middle
The newsletter and the Brain Health Playbook.
Bottom
The assessment, then clinicians once it was repositioned for clinics.
$1M raised across Shark Tank India and Meet the Drapers.

India's first gamified early-detection platform for brain health.

What to take from it

In a category people avoid, trust is the growth channel. The ten-minute cap was the most commercial decision we made, precisely because it looked like the least.
